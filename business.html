Business model · roadmap · proposal §9–§14

Software revenue, not chargers.

ReachGrid is a B2B/B2G geospatial decision-support product. It doesn't own stations or take a share of electricity sales. Every price, count and rate below is a hypothesis to test with customers, not an observed market figure. The calculators let you replace them with validated numbers.

Scenarios here are arithmetic, not forecasts. Annualised revenue is not profit and not cash collected. Public-sector procurement cycles are long and are not treated as guaranteed revenue.

Bottom-up market sizing

The proposal rejects inventing a TAM from an arbitrary count × an arbitrary price. This calculator only produces a figure once you enter verified counts of eligible organisations per segment and a validated willingness-to-pay range. Overlap between segments and procurement constraints are removed explicitly.

TAM = Σ eligible organisations × validated WTP × (1 − overlap)

Why a customer might pay

  • Less analyst time merging fragmented datasets and producing repeated regional reports. One methodology covers all 737 districts.
  • Candidate sites compared before money is spent on surveys, engineering studies or property negotiations.
  • A documented, reproducible rationale for proposals and budget allocation (scenario IDs, audit log, provenance).
  • Gaps tracked and plans updated as registrations, chargers or roads change (weekly refresh, change report).
  • Portfolio coverage compared across geographies with one consistent method.

Go-to-market

  1. Start with a narrow pilot geography and a clearly defined buyer problem.
  2. Run structured interviews with CPOs, fleet operators, consultants and planners to test whether site screening is a budgeted problem.
  3. Offer a limited proof-of-value study on public data with a clear methodology. The workspace already produces one for any district.
  4. Measure time saved, decision usefulness and willingness to pay for updates.
  5. Convert repeat workflows into annual subscriptions; use custom consulting only where it builds reusable product.
  6. Partner with data providers, engineering consultants and host-site networks after the core workflow is proven.

Segments

A: CPOs and charging networks · B: fleet, logistics and mobility operators · C: real-estate and destination hosts · D: consulting, engineering and planning firms · E: government and public-sector buyers (modelled separately because of tender cycles).

Engineering principles in the code

  • Ingestion adapters (pipeline/) are separate from the analytical engine (js/engine.js, js/advanced.js).
  • Spatial grid indexes, and precomputed demand cells and coverage, are stored per build.
  • Source facts, derived features, assumptions and scenario outputs are kept separate. Scenario IDs hash every input.
  • Heavy jobs (ILP, sensitivity, backtest) run asynchronously in a Web Worker; the API returns stored results.
